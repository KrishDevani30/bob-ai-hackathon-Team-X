# Problem Statement

## Background

Clinical research and pharmaceutical development depend on reliable protocol execution across many investigational sites. A Phase III oncology trial can generate thousands of patient visits, dosing records, concomitant-medication records, and safety assessments, creating a large and distributed compliance-monitoring workload.

## The Problem

Protocol deviations such as missed or out-of-window visits, incorrect dosing, prohibited medications, missing safety assessments, late data entry, and eligibility violations can remain hidden until a monitoring visit or regulatory audit. Without early site-level risk visibility, teams may discover repeated deviations only after they affect patient safety, data integrity, or submission readiness.

## Who is Affected

The primary users are clinical trial risk managers, clinical operations leaders, clinical research associates, and data-quality teams responsible for monitoring investigational sites. They need to review deviations by site and patient, understand severity and recurrence, and prioritize corrective and preventive actions.

## Why It Matters

Late detection increases manual review effort, creates avoidable site follow-up, and can compromise patient-safety oversight and the reliability of trial data. In a large trial, unresolved findings can contribute to audit observations, rejected or delayed submissions, and substantial financial impact; the project documentation uses an illustrative estimate of 6–12 months of delay and $50–100M in cost for a rejected submission.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Teams commonly rely on periodic monitoring visits, spreadsheets, manual data review, and separate clinical-data systems. These approaches are useful for detailed review but do not provide a single, continuously recalculated view of protocol findings, severity, recurrence, and site risk. They also make it harder to apply consistent detection rules and prioritize the sites that need attention first.
